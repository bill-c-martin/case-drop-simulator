# Beast Case Drop Lab

An interactive screening model for a B.C. Rich Beast in a Rockcase RC 10625B, with bottom and lid polyurethane (PU) sheets and a polyethylene (PE) body cradle.

Run `npm install` and `npm run dev`. Run `npm test` for the energy and contact checks, or `npm run build` for a static site in `dist/`.

The six landing buttons set base, lid, left side, right side, headstock end, or feet/tail end impacts. The tilt and edge-direction sliders remain available for angles between those presets. Each PU sheet has an independent ½″, ¾″, or 1″ thickness control. The `?` buttons define the inputs, units, and results in plain English.

## What is measured and what is estimated

- The [Thomann RC 10625B listing](https://www.thomann.co.uk/rockcase_rc10625b_beast_jrv_case.htm) states 121 × 45 × 8 cm overall dimensions, a 5 mm wood shell, 7 kg case weight, and compatibility with the Beast, JrV and RR. A [Music Store listing](https://www.musicstore.com/de_LU/EUR/Rockcase-RC-10625-Case-fuer-Beast-JR-V-Black/art-GIT0007359-000) gives a different 119.5 × 46 × 8 cm *interior* figure. The starting interior dimensions use the latter length and width. Check your case: retailer dimensions are inconsistent and the fit changes near the wings.
- The [2002 B.C. Rich catalog](https://guitar-compare.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/2002_B.C.Rich_Catalog.pdf) lists an NJ Beast. A [listed NJ neck-through example](https://empireguitars.com/products/beast-nj-neck-through-b-c-rich-1) weighs 9 lb 4 oz and has a 25.5-inch scale. This simulation uses the user's 10 lb weight. I did not find a reliable factory body outline or complete dimensions for a 2000s NJ Beast. The outline is a hand trace from a [Beast NT photo](https://www.guitarcenter.com/Used/BC-Rich/Used-BC-Rich-BEAST-NT-Black-Solid-Body-Electric-Guitar.gc), and its default 44.5-inch overall length, 17.2-inch body width, and 1.75-inch body thickness are *estimates*. Enter measurements of your own instrument before using the map as a cutting guide.
- The default PU curve uses [H-O Products' 1.2 pcf polyether PU data](https://h-oproducts.com/product/1-2-polyether-polyurethane-foam/): 0.45 psi at 25%, 0.65 psi at 50%, and 1.1 psi at 70% compression. A [1.4–1.5 pcf polyether PU grade](https://www.foamparts.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/1715.pdf) exists, but its public sheet does not give an equivalent compression stress curve. If you use that density, enter the specific sheet's compression values. Density does not uniquely determine stiffness.
- The default 1.75 pcf PE curve uses [LAMATEK's 25% point](https://lamatek.com/data-sheets/closed-cell/nxl-series-1-7lb-non-xl-pe/) of 7.5 psi. Its 50% point is not published on that page; the default 14 psi is an illustrative estimate informed by a separate [2.0 pcf PE sheet](https://www.thefoamfactory.com/tech/polyethylene-foam-roll-Tech.html), which reports 5 psi at 25% and 14 psi at 50%. The 2.0 pcf preset uses those two values. Confirm the actual PE product grade and its curve before relying on the numbers.

## Physics model

For each selected impact direction, the model projects rigid-guitar motion onto approximately 200 body-face cells and each short segment of the traced body boundary. Face cells use only the impacted PU sheet because the guitar sits in the PE cutout. Edge cells use PE; their available sideward compression thickness is the distance from that edge to the case shell, and their vertical contact height is limited by the PE cradle height. Only PE webs within the adjustable backed-span limit count as supported. The neck-support and headstock contact areas are approximate; the bridge and pickup heights are simplified local raised areas on lid drops. Headstock-first drops also include an assumed 0.25-inch existing end liner, with its thickness editable and its response approximated by the soft PU curve. The actual case-end padding material is not specified in the listings.

Stress between supplied compression points is interpolated. An illustrative steep curve extends beyond the published 70% PU and 50% PE values, with a warning. The model integrates `area × thickness × ∫ stress d(strain)` until it equals the gravitational drop energy `weight × height`. It reports the summed force component along impact and divides by instrument weight for deceleration in g. If any modeled contact reaches 100% compression before absorbing the energy, it stops there, reports the energy remaining, and marks force and g as lower bounds. The shell is treated as rigid. The [Canadian Conservation Institute cushioning guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/conservation-preservation-publications/technical-bulletins/effective-packaging-transport-artwork.html) informs the use of energy, static loading, and compression strain for cushioning, but does not validate this particular guitar model.

This is a design exploration tool. It omits case flex, rebound, rate-dependent foam behavior, latch and hinge loads, guitar bending, and damage thresholds. The pressure map is pressure on the foam, not stress inside the guitar. Measure the fit, inspect the actual foam datasheets, and use instrumented drop testing for protection decisions.
